How to read your ads report: the numbers that actually matter

A report full of reach, impressions and clicks, and you still can't tell whether your ads work. Which numbers to read first, which to ignore, and what to ask whoever runs your ads.

Your monthly report arrives: reach 180,000, impressions 420,000, click-through rate 2.1%. The numbers are big and colorful, and there's a green arrow at the bottom. Then you ask the only question you care about: "Did I get customers?" And the report has no answer.

This article isn't for the person running the ads. It's for you, the business owner who pays and wants to understand.

Two kinds of numbers

Every number in the report falls into one of two groups:

Reach numbers: reach, impressions, views, engagement. They tell you how many people saw the ad. Useful to whoever runs the campaign, but they tell you nothing about sales.

Result numbers: messages, bookings, orders, and the cost of each. These are the numbers that belong to you.

The simple rule: read the result numbers first. If they're good, modest reach doesn't matter much. If they're bad, no amount of reach makes up for it.

The numbers to read first

1. Number of results

How many messages, bookings or orders came from the ads? This number should be at the top of the report, not buried at the end.

2. Cost per result

Divide what you spent by the number of results. Spend 10,000 and get 200 messages, and each message cost 50.

3. Cost per customer

This is the most important number, and it's usually missing from the report because it needs data from you. Of those 200 messages, how many people actually bought? If 20 did, each customer cost 500.

Now compare it with what one customer earns you. If a customer is worth 2,000 to you, the ads work. If a customer is worth 300, the campaign loses money however good the other numbers look.

Why you need your own numbers

The platform knows who clicked and who sent a message. It doesn't know who bought from you after the conversation. That number sits with you, or with your sales team.

So the best report is built together: platform numbers on one side, your sales numbers on the other. Without the second half, the report tells half a story.

A simple way to start: ask your team to ask every new customer one question, "How did you hear about us?", and log the answer in a simple sheet.

Questions to ask whoever runs your ads

  1. What is the single goal of each campaign? Messages, bookings, or site visits?
  2. What did each result cost this month compared with last month? Why did it change?
  3. Which ad brought the best results, which did we pause, and why?
  4. What will you test next month, and how will we know if it worked?

Someone who runs your ads with real understanding will answer these easily. Someone who lives on reach numbers will avoid them.

The report you deserve: one page

A good report doesn't need twenty pages. One page is enough if it answers the right questions in this order: results this month against last month; what each result cost and why it moved; how many of those results bought, and what each customer cost (this part needs your numbers); what worked; what was paused and why; and one clear test for next month with how it will be judged. Reach numbers belong in an appendix for anyone who wants detail. The first page is yours, and it answers one question: is what you pay coming back to you?

Warning signs in a report

  • No result count at all: every number is about reach and views.
  • The goal changes every month: engagement one month, views the next, messages after that, so nothing can be compared.
  • "Reach doubled" as the headline: reach is a means, not a result.
  • No recommendations: a report that describes what happened without saying what changes next.

Common questions

What is a "good" cost per message? There's no single number. It varies by field, country and product. The real test is whether a customer costs less than they earn you.

Should I stop a campaign if the cost per result is high? Not right away. Find the cause first: is it the ad, the audience, or how messages are handled? Sometimes the ad is fine and the messages get lost after they arrive.

How often should I check the report? A quick weekly look at result numbers, and a deeper review once a month.

The short version

  • Read result numbers before reach numbers.
  • Work out the cost per customer, not per click, and compare it with what a customer earns you.
  • The best report combines platform numbers with your sales numbers.
  • Ask four questions, and notice who avoids them.

Try it on your ads

Take the last report you received and look for one number: how many customers came from ads? If you can't find it, that's the first thing to ask for in the next report.
